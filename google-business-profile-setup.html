Google Business Profile Setup: A Step-by-Step Guide

By Velrixa Studio · Digital Growth Strategy

Key takeaways

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that puts your business on Google Search and Google Maps, with your hours, phone number, reviews, photos and directions in one place. Set it up in order: create or claim the profile with your exact business name, choose the most accurate primary category, add your services, photos and description, set your hours, collect reviews, and complete verification. Then keep it active with weekly review replies and a post or photo at least once a month. A complete, verified, actively maintained profile is the foundation of local search visibility — and the first thing most customers see before they ever visit your website.

When a customer searches for a plumber, a dentist, a cafe or a designer near them, the first thing they usually see is not a website — it is a Google Business Profile: the business name, the star rating, the opening hours, the phone number, and a map pin, all in one small panel. That panel decides who gets the call. A business without a profile, or with a half-finished one, simply does not appear in that moment. Setting up a Google Business Profile is free, and it takes an afternoon to do properly. This guide walks through the whole setup in order — creating or claiming the profile, categories, services, photos, reviews, posts, questions and answers, and verification — with the mistakes to avoid at each step. Step 1: Create or Claim Your Profile

Start by searching for your business name on Google Maps. If a profile already exists — because a customer, a directory, or Google itself created one — claim it rather than creating a second one. Duplicate profiles split your reviews and confuse customers, and they are a common problem for businesses that have been around for a while. If no profile exists, create one at Google Business Profile using a Google account that belongs to the business, not a personal account you might lose access to, and not an account owned by a single employee. Enter the exact business name — your real trading name, with no extra keywords stuffed in. Google removes or penalises listings that add keywords to the business name, so "Smith Plumbing" is correct and "Smith Plumbing — Best Plumber in Leeds, Cheap Emergency Repairs" is not. Choose whether customers visit you at a location, you visit them, or both. A shop, cafe or clinic shows its address; a service-area business such as a plumber or a designer can hide its address and show only the areas it serves. Enter the address and phone number exactly as they appear everywhere else your business is listed — consistency matters, because the same details on your profile, your website and your directories are what tells Google the listing is real. Add your website address, and double-check that the link works before you move on.

Step 2: Categories, Services and Business Details

Categories are the most important choice in the whole setup, because they decide which searches your business appears for. Choose one primary category — the most accurate description of what you actually do — and then add the useful secondary categories. A bakery that also serves coffee might use "Bakery" as the primary category and "Cafe" as a secondary one. Do not add categories for services you do not offer just to appear in more searches: a wrong category brings the wrong customers, who leave without calling, and it can get the profile flagged. Next, fill in the services, products or menu where they apply. A service business should list each service with a short description and a price where it makes sense; a restaurant should fill in its menu; a shop should list its main product ranges. Write the business description in plain words: what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different — using the words customers actually search for, without stuffing keywords into every sentence. Set your opening hours carefully, including any split hours, and add special hours for holidays in advance, because nothing frustrates a customer more than driving to a business that Google said was open. Add the attributes that apply — wheelchair access, outdoor seating, free Wi-Fi, appointment-only — because customers filter by them, and a missing attribute can hide you from a search you would otherwise appear in.

Step 3: Photos That Make the Profile Work

Photos are the first thing a searcher looks at after the star rating, and a profile with no photos looks abandoned. Add a logo and a cover photo first, then at least ten photos of the real business: the outside of the premises so customers can recognise it, the inside, the team at work, and the finished work or products. Use clear, well-lit photos taken recently — not stock photos of a different business, and not photos from five years ago showing a shop that has since been redecorated. Add new photos regularly, at least once a month, because fresh photos signal that the business is active. If you sell food, photograph the dishes you actually serve; if you are a tradesperson, photograph completed jobs with the customer's permission; if you are a designer or a consultant, photograph your workspace and your work. Avoid photos with text overlaid on them — sale banners, phone numbers, discount messages — because they clutter the listing and look unprofessional. And check the profile on a phone after you upload, because that is how most local searchers see it: do the photos look good at a small size, and does the first photo represent the business well?

Step 4: Reviews — Getting Them and Replying to Them

Reviews affect both how your business shows up in local results and whether a searcher chooses you over the business next door, so build the review habit from day one. Google gives you a short review link for your profile — use it. Send it to happy customers after the job is done: a short message, a card at the till, or a link in the receipt email. Ask politely and once, at the right time — a day or two after the service, when the customer is happy and the experience is fresh. Do not offer payment or discounts in exchange for reviews, and do not ask only for five-star reviews: ask for an honest review, and let the service earn the stars. Reply to every review, including the negative ones, politely and specifically. A reply that says "Thanks for your feedback, we have fixed the scheduling issue you mentioned" shows future customers that the business listens and improves. A calm, helpful reply to a bad review can turn a warning into a recommendation, while an unanswered bad review just sits there. Never argue with a reviewer in public, and never post fake reviews — Google removes them, and a business caught doing it loses trust it cannot buy back. Read the reviews for patterns too: if three customers mention the same problem, that is a business problem to fix at the source, not a marketing problem.

Step 5: Posts, Q&A and Keeping the Profile Active

A Google Business Profile is not a set-and-forget listing; it rewards businesses that stay active. Posts are short updates that appear on your profile — an offer, a new service, a seasonal note, a photo of recent work — and they show searchers that the business is open, busy and current. Add a post at least once a month, and more often when you have something real to say: a holiday opening announcement, a new product, or a limited offer with a clear end date. Keep posts short and specific, with one call to action — call, book, visit — and a real photo rather than a stock image. The questions and answers section needs attention too. Customers ask questions directly on your profile, and anyone can answer them — including competitors and random users — so check the section weekly, answer new questions yourself, and add the questions customers ask most often so your own answers are already there. A profile with recent posts, answered questions and replied-to reviews tells both Google and customers that the business is alive. A profile untouched for six months tells the opposite story.

Step 6: Verification — and the Mistakes to Avoid

Verification is the step that proves to Google that the business is real and that you are authorised to manage it, and the profile has limited visibility until it is complete. The method Google offers depends on the business type and location. Instant or phone verification takes minutes where it is offered. A verification postcard by mail usually takes around one to two weeks to arrive, and you then enter the code printed on the card — do not request a second card while the first is on its way, because only the newest code works. Video verification, where Google asks you to record a short video showing your business, your signage and your workspace, usually takes a few days to review once submitted. Whichever method you get, follow the instructions exactly, and keep the profile information unchanged while verification is in progress, because edits can restart the process. The mistakes to avoid across the whole setup are simple: never add keywords to your business name, never create a second profile when one already exists, never use a virtual office or a PO box as your address, never buy reviews, and never let the profile go stale. Set a weekly reminder — ten minutes to check reviews, questions and hours — and a quarterly reminder to review the categories, services and description. That small routine is the difference between a profile that brings in calls and one that quietly disappears from local results.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. Google may offer optional paid advertising alongside it, but the profile itself — the listing, the map pin, the reviews, the photos and the posts — costs nothing. Any service that charges you to create the profile is charging for its own time, not for a Google fee.

How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

It depends on the verification method. Instant or phone verification can take minutes where it is offered. A verification postcard by mail usually takes around one to two weeks to arrive, and you then enter the code from the card. Video verification, where Google asks you to record a short video of your business, usually takes a few days to review once submitted.

Can I set up a Google Business Profile without a shopfront?

Yes. Service-area businesses — plumbers, cleaners, consultants, designers and others who visit customers — can set up a profile without showing a public address. You still need a real business address for verification, but you can choose to hide it and show only the areas you serve instead.

How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Check it weekly: reply to new reviews and questions, and add a post or a photo at least once a month so the profile looks active. Update the hours immediately whenever they change, including holiday hours, and review the categories, services and description every three months to make sure they still match what the business actually offers.
